PlayBill Display

your Playbills, on the wall again. Photograph your Playbills, drop the photos into a little Mac app, and a jailbroken Kindle shows them on its e-ink screen: a different show each day, or whichever one you pick, synced over Wi-Fi.

View on GitHub How it's built

A stack of Playbills in a drawer is a collection nobody sees.

Every show you've seen leaves one behind. This puts them back on display, one at a time, on a screen that looks like paper, needs no backlight, and sips power. The Mac keeps the archive: who you went with, who signed it at the stage door, and the whole cast, read straight off the page.

✺ what it does

Cuts out the Playbill

Snap it on any table. The app finds the four corners, straightens out the tilt and perspective, and keeps only the Playbill. Drag the corners yourself when it guesses wrong.

Keeps the signatures

A blue Sharpie on a gray cover vanishes in plain grayscale. The e-ink conversion boosts ink that differs in color from the cover, so autographs survive the trip down to 16 grays.

Reads the cast list

Photograph the cast page and the names come back as Role — Actor lines, even from a crooked photo. Show and theatre are read off the cover. All on the Mac, using macOS's built-in text recognition.

You pick what's up

A library in the browser shows exactly what's on the Kindle. Show any Playbill, keep one up for a week, or let it rotate daily. The Kindle switches within a minute.

✺ architecture

Two small programs that talk over your Wi-Fi. The Mac does all the heavy lifting: image processing, text recognition, the library. The Kindle stays dumb on purpose: a shell script that cron runs every minute, which pulls from the Mac, so a sleeping Mac or a dropped connection never leaves it stuck.

And what happens to a photo on its way to the screen:

  1. Find the pageBrightness and color edges, straight-line hypotheses, scored by edge support and the Playbill's 5⅜ × 8½ shape.
  2. StraightenPerspective warp of the four corners into a flat rectangle, trimmed a hair at the edges.
  3. Boost the inkPixels whose color differs from their local surroundings get pushed away from the background's brightness.
  4. Lift detailGentle local contrast (CLAHE), then an unsharp mask sized to stroke width.
  5. 16 graysQuantized to the panel's exact gray levels, dithered or crisp. Out comes a 1236 × 1648 PNG.
✺ learnings

Check what your tools actually do

The first plan scheduled the display script with Jobman. Jobman turned out to be a jailbreak manager (SSH, recovery, update blocking) with no scheduler at all. The Kindle had cron the whole time. A five-minute look at the README would have saved a confused round trip.

Let the small device pull

The Kindle asks the Mac every minute rather than the Mac pushing to it. Requests carry a sequence number, so "show this one" is applied exactly once even if the Kindle hears it ten times. When the Mac is asleep, the Kindle shrugs and keeps rotating what it has.

Delete last

An early sync downloaded new images and then removed old ones, even if the downloads had failed. One app restart mid-sync and the Kindle had nothing left to show. The fix is old advice: only clean up once every new file has arrived.

Grayscale throws away exactly what you care about

Blue marker on a gray cover has the same brightness, so a plain grayscale conversion erases the autograph. Looking at color difference against each pixel's neighborhood, in Lab color space, brings it back without making the whole cover noisy.

The same trap showed up in page detection: a yellow banner overlapping a white page is a strong edge to the eye and nearly invisible in grayscale.

Synthetic tests pass; real photos teach

Corner detection worked on every test photo I generated, then failed on the first real one: a second Playbill peeking out behind, a yellow band, a curled page. Generated data is good for regressions. The real photo is the spec.

Domain knowledge beats heuristics

Text recognition read the title as "The Play That Goe Wronc," and I tried to explain it away as a curled page. It's The Play That Goes Wrong; the cut-off letters are the joke on the cover. No algorithm was going to know that.

Embedded Linux is full of small floors

Cron runs with a minimal PATH, so the drawing tool in /usr/sbin vanished. macOS sprinkles ._ metadata files on FAT drives, which match *.png. And while the Kindle is mounted over USB, its own storage disappears from under the running script. Each one is a line of defensive shell.

On-device beats the cloud for this

macOS's Vision framework, called from Python through PyObjC, reads cover titles and cast pages in under a second, offline, with nothing uploaded. Its one quirk: hand it the photo with the phone's rotation already applied, then try a few small tilts so roles and names land on the same row.

✺ what you'll need
  • A Mac, with uv installed.
  • A jailbroken Kindle with SSH. Built on a Paperwhite 11th gen with Jobman; other models work with their screen size set.
  • Wi-Fi, and a charger for the Kindle: it stays awake to keep the Playbill up.

Setup, from clone to the first Playbill on screen, is in the README.
